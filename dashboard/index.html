How the model works

One formula scores every protocol. Nothing is hardcoded by name: the category multiples are computed from this snapshot's own live-token protocols at run time.

implied = TVL × category multiple × growth × maturity × chains
  • Category multiple — the median market-cap-to-TVL ratio of live-token protocols in the same category (at least 5 of them), otherwise the overall median. Not clamped: the held-out test below showed clamping the multiples hurts.
  • Growth — 1 + clamp(7-day TVL change, −60%, +120%) × 0.6.
  • Maturity — 0.6 + min(age, 1095 days) / 1095 × 0.4. Older with no token yet counts for more; unknown age counts as 365 days.
  • Chains — 1 + 0.01 per extra chain, up to 10 chains.
  • Uncertainty range — the same formula with the category's 25th and 75th percentile multiples instead of the median. The range is wide because market-cap-to-TVL varies a lot inside a category; the dashboard shows it on every row's detail view.
  • Launch odds — the share of protocols in the same category that already have a token, smoothed, discounted by age. A base rate from the peers' record, not a prediction of whether or when a token is issued.

Held-out test — the model is tested, not just asserted

The live protocols are split in two deterministic ways and the full model is fitted on one half and asked to predict the other half's market caps. The numbers are the median miss on the half the model never saw.

The miss is large — around 90% at the median — because market-cap-to-TVL varies by more than a hundredfold inside a single category, so no multiple of TVL can price a live token closely. The category multiples still beat one flat multiple on one split (127% → 99% median miss). Growth, maturity and chains are kept because the radar's job is ranking launch candidates, not pricing live tokens: a growing, young protocol is a better candidate even though growth does not predict a live token's market cap.

What “pre-token” means

A protocol is listed as pre-token when its DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol. That is what the record says — never proof it has no token. A protocol on the list may still have a token its record does not carry. The board never predicts whether or when a token is issued; the implied value is this entry's own claim about what a launch would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is.

The board also leaves out wrapped, staked and bridged products of tokens that already trade — the same exclusion the snapshot's own watchlist rule uses (Bridge, Liquid Staking and related categories, names starting with an exchange's name, names carrying “wrapped”, “staked” or “staking”, and versioned pools such as “V2”). A launch candidate has to be a protocol of its own, not a wrapper around someone else's token. Those rows are listed on the Not candidates tab with the reason.

How the model works

One rule scores every candidate: the fitted curve's launch value, times the launch odds from the peers' record. Nothing is hardcoded by name: the curve and the odds are fitted from this snapshot's own protocols at run time.

implied value = launch value × launch odds

  • Launch value — the power curve fitted on the 92 traded tokens whose market cap the snapshot can price and that are not themselves a product of another token: log(market cap) = intercept + slope × log(TVL). On this snapshot the slope is 0.39, so market cap grows more slowly than TVL: a $100M protocol prices near $12.9M and a $2.75B protocol near $47.6M. The old rule, each category's median market-cap-to-TVL ratio, prices the same $2.75B protocol near $510M; the blind test below shows why that overprices it, and the multiples table at the bottom of this tab lets a reader see what the old rule would have said for every category.
  • Launch odds — the forward two-year hazard from the peers' record. A logistic curve fitted on the 442 peers (every protocol that is not itself a product) gives F(age), the share of peers that record a token by a given age. The odds for a candidate of age a are [F(a + 2 years) − F(a)] / [1 − F(a)]: the share of the peers that reach age a with no token and record one in the next two years. Young protocols get high odds and old ones low odds, because the peers' record says so — the table below shows the fitted curve against the raw age buckets.
  • Uncertainty range — the curve's own held-out error: the 25th and 75th percentile (and the 10th and 90th) of the leave-one-out log-residuals, applied to the row's implied value. The range-honesty table checks how often a real market cap lands inside the printed range.
  • Value terms — added in order and kept only when BOTH blind tests improve: the leave-one-out test (each of the 92 traded tokens priced from a fit on the other 91) and the walk-forward test (the older half of the traded tokens, listed before the median listing date, prices the newer half). The size curve beats the flat multiple on both. The category premium, 7-day growth, years listed and the carry term do not, and are tested, not kept. The chain count improves both by a hair and is kept. The full ablation table is on the board page and below.
  • Farm verdict — the plan's economics at a stated scenario: a $10,000 deposit, a 7% drop share, 12 weeks held. The expected drop is the implied value times the drop share times the farmer's share of the pool, deposit / (TVL + deposit). The cost is gas for every on-chain step at the chain's stated rate (Ethereum $5, BSC $0.10, Solana $0.05, other chains $0.50 a step), plus the capital charge of locking the deposit at 2% a year. The verdict compares the net with a 4% T-bill on the same money and with $25 an hour of hands-on work: FARM when the net beats the T-bill and clears $25 an hour, MARGINAL when it is positive but below that bar, SKIP when it is not. The scenario controls on the Board tab re-price every verdict in place.
  • Integrity seal — the board's seal is a checksum of its ranks, slugs and implied values, computed when the board is written and recomputed by this page from the same data. If the two disagree, the strip at the top says so.

Held-out test — the model is tested, not just asserted

What “pre-token” means

A protocol is listed as pre-token when its DefiLlama record carries no market cap, no CoinGecko id and no symbol. That is what the record says — never proof it has no token. A protocol on the list may still have a token its record does not carry. The board never predicts whether or when a token is issued; the implied value is this entry's own claim about what a launch would be worth if the protocol's record stayed as it is, weighted by how often its peers record one.

The board ranks the 198 launch candidates, not all 758 protocols: a protocol whose token already trades is a comparison, not a candidate, and a wrapped, staked, bridged, exchange or versioned product is set aside with its reason. The ledger at the top of the page accounts for every one of the 758 protocols in exactly one group.
